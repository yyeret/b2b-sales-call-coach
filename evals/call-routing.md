# Call routing checks

All snippets are synthetic. Run each transcript independently with `sales-call-analyzer`; compare its eligibility, observed stage and decision to continue with the expectations below. These checks validate judgment and stopping behavior, not exact wording. Do not infer a complete deal history from a short transcript.

| Case | Transcript | Expected behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal pipeline review | Manager: “Which opportunities will close this quarter?” Rep: “The prospect asked for a discount. I will call them tomorrow.” | Not a sales motion: internal sales management. Stop before coaching, scores or buyer follow-up. Discussing sales is not itself a buyer conversation. |
| Customer delivery kickoff | Customer: “The contract is signed. Today we should agree deployment access and introduce the implementation team.” Delivery lead: “Let's confirm the launch checklist.” | Not a sales motion: delivery. Stop; do not manufacture an upsell because a customer attends. |
| Support incident | Customer: “Our export failed this morning.” Support rep: “Send the job ID so we can diagnose it.” | Not a sales motion: support. Stop without treating missing budget, urgency or economic-buyer access as sales failures. |
| Candidate interview | Recruiter: “Tell me about your Terraform experience and salary expectations.” Candidate: “I maintained our build pipelines.” | Not a sales motion: candidate evaluation. Stop; hiring vocabulary and a commercial employer do not establish a sales opportunity. |
| Early introduction | Seller: “We offer warehouse safety assessments. What prompted you to speak with us?” Buyer: “I'm exploring whether you can help our new site. Can we set a discovery meeting for Tuesday?” | Sales motion, introduction. Continue with introduction criteria; do not demand a complete procurement map or claim the deal is commercially qualified. |
| Existing-account staffing need | Customer: “We already use your help desk. Can you also provide a backend engineer for our new product?” Seller: “Let's understand the responsibilities, timing and how you will choose the supplier.” | Sales motion, existing-account expansion, discovery/qualification. Continue; staffing vocabulary does not make this a candidate interview. |
| Pre-purchase technical evaluation | Buyer: “Your proposal is on our shortlist. Before choosing a vendor, we need to test the integration's failure recovery.” Seller engineer: “Let's agree the acceptance criteria for this test.” | Sales motion, solution/technical evaluation. Continue with evaluation criteria; do not classify it as delivery or penalize it for not repeating discovery. |
| Small talk only | A: “Can you hear me?” B: “Yes. We are still waiting for the others.” | Insufficient evidence. Stop without classifying it as confirmed non-sales or producing scores. |
| Mixed delivery and expansion | 00:00 Customer: “Let's review the rollout of the service we bought.” 10:00 Customer: “Separate topic: we want a quote to add two sites. Our purchasing lead will join a scoping call Friday.” | Mixed call with an eligible expansion segment from 10:00. Analyze only that segment; rep performance is Not assessed because no seller behavior was supplied. No aggregate score. Do not use delivery discussion as failed discovery. |
| Sales intent, unclear stage | Buyer: “This conversation is about potentially buying your service.” Seller: “Let's continue from last time.” | Sales eligibility is confirmed, but stage is unresolved. Return classification and missing stage context; stop before stage-dependent scores and a deal verdict. |

Cross-case checks: preserve unknowns; cite actual transcript evidence; keep positioning unscored without supplied criteria; distinguish seller activity from a dated buyer commitment.
